# Searches — what we are actually doing, in configuration space

> A **search** moves a point around configuration space `C = ℝ³ⱽ` until it lands where we want:
> on a thin manifold (flat, maybe a fixed modulus) and inside a tiny open set (embedded). This
> document is the geometric picture — what each search is doing to the point — and the one
> conceptual fork that matters: solving *for* a modulus vs *marching* to it.
> Code: `src/search/` (built on `src/solvers/` + the conditions).

## The landscape in `C`

A configuration is a point in `C = ℝ³ⱽ` (for V=8, ℝ²⁴). Three features carve it up:

- **The flat manifold `F`.** "Every cone angle = 2π" is `V−1` equations (Gauss–Bonnet drops one), so
  the flat configurations form a **curved surface `F` of dimension 3V − (V−1) = 2V+1 = 17** in C.
- **The modulus foliation.** On `F`, the modulus `τ̂` is a (locally smooth) function. Fixing one
  coordinate — say `Re τ̂ = s` — is one more equation, slicing `F` into a stack of **16-dimensional
  leaves `Mₛ = {flat, Re τ̂ = s}`**, one per shape-value `s`. Fixing the *whole* `τ̂` (codim 2) cuts
  down to a 15-dim point-modulus locus.
- **The embedded region `Ω`.** "No two triangles cross" is an **open** condition — a blob `Ω ⊆ C`
  with a fuzzy, non-smooth boundary. It is *not* a surface; you stay *inside* it, you don't land *on* it.

The objects we hunt are the **flat embedded tori** — points in `F ∩ Ω` — and the *special* ones live
where a modulus leaf meets the blob: `Mₛ ∩ Ω`.

## Three ways to move a point (the solvers)

All three are generated by the same constraint Jacobian (one J-hub); they differ in *how* they move:

- **`project`** — a **perpendicular hop onto a leaf**. Min-norm Gauss–Newton: from a nearby point it
  jumps the shortest way onto `{g = 0}` (onto `F`, or onto a leaf `Mₛ`). One landing.
- **`flow`** — a **slide *along* a leaf, into `Ω`**. Riemannian descent of a repulsion energy whose
  gradient is tangent-projected onto the leaf, so the point moves *within* `F` (or `Mₛ`) toward
  embeddedness, gated to stay in `Ω`. It changes *where on the leaf* you are, not *which* leaf.
- **`march`** — a **walk across the stack of leaves, staying in `Ω`**. Continuation: creep the target
  `s`, `project` onto the next leaf, gate `Ω`, repeat (see below).

## The searches

Each search is a motion (or two) composed with `certify`:

- **`discover`** — *any* flat embedded torus. `project` onto `F`, then `flow` into `Ω`. The point
  lands somewhere on `F` and slides until embedded; whatever leaf it ends on is its modulus.
  `held = [flat]`.
- **`wall`** — flat embedded tori on a chosen leaf `|Re τ̂| = c` (rectangular `c=0`, rhombic `c=½`).
  `project` onto the leaf `Mₐ` directly (flat **and** the modulus, in one solve), then `flow` into `Ω`
  *along that leaf*. `held = [flat, modulusWall(c)]`.
- **`semiSolution`** — flat *immersions* of the Doyle–Schwartz structure. `project` in a **pinned
  coordinate system** (the six base vertices frozen to z=0) onto `F ∧ {two triples collinear}`. No `Ω`
  gate — "semi" means embeddedness is *recorded*, not required. `held = [flat, collinear, collinear]`.
- **`march` to a modulus** — *transport* a flat embedded torus from its leaf to a far one, mapping
  where the path pinches. The motion below.

## The fork: solving *for* a modulus vs *marching* to it

Both the `wall` solve and `march` pin the modulus — and **both Newton-solve the full `[flat, modulus]`
system**, driving `Re τ̂` to its target value exactly. The difference is not *which* constraints they
enforce; it is whether they reach the target **leaf in one jump or by walking**.

**Direct (`wall`): one perpendicular hop to the target leaf.** `project([flat, modulusWall(c)])` lands
on `Mₐ` in a single min-norm solve, with the SL(2,ℤ) reducing matrix `m` **frozen once at the seed**.
Embeddedness is handled *after* (the accept test) or *locally* (`flow`). It works only when the target
leaf is **near** the seed — same SL(2,ℤ) chamber, and the straight jump lands inside `Ω`. It answers
*"is there a flat embedded torus at this shape, near where I already am?"* — the tool to **densely
sample a locus you can already reach** (perturb an on-leaf torus, re-project; one cheap solve each).

**`march`: crawl leaf-by-leaf, gating `Ω` every step.** Creep the target `s` a hair toward the goal,
`project([flat, modulusWall(s)])` onto the next leaf, check `isEmbedded`, accept and grow the step or
back off and halve it; repeat. The modulus is solved **exactly at every step** — what marches is the
target value `s`, not the constraint set. We step (rather than jump) for three reasons, all about the
*path* not the *solve*:

1. **the `Ω` gate** — small steps let us catch the embedded boundary and re-route before overshooting out;
2. **the frozen chart** — `m` is **re-frozen each step**, so the modulus constraint stays the *true*
   reduced one as the point drifts across SL(2,ℤ) chambers (a far jump would use a stale `m`);
3. **conditioning** — each `project` starts from the previous solution, a tiny well-behaved correction
   instead of a wild long-range Newton solve.

When feasible steps shrink to nothing short of the target, the path is **pinched** — `march` returns
`blocked` with the modulus it reached: *the extremal shape where `Ω` closes*, the boundary of the
realizable region (which the direct solve can't report — it just fails). So `march` answers *"how far
can I push the shape before embeddability runs out, and what's the torus all the way there?"* — the
tool to **reach a new shape**, **navigate around embeddedness obstacles** a straight jump would crash
into, and **map the edge** of what's embeddable.

**In one line:** direct = *"solve for a torus at this shape (if it's close)"*; march = *"carry a torus
to a new shape and discover where it stops being possible."*

## In code

| search | motion | held | coordinate system | gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `discover` | project → flow | `[flat]` | `fullSpace` | embedded |
| `wall(c)` | project → flow | `[flat, modulusWall(c)]` | `fullSpace` | embedded |
| `semiSolution` | project | `[flat, collinear, collinear]` | `pinCoords(baseZ)` | — (records it) |
| `march`-modulus | project-continuation | `[flat, modulusWall(s)]` per step | `fullSpace` | embedded |

Every search builds a `ConfigSpace`, `pull`s its `held` conditions into it (`search/pull.ts`), and runs
the ℝⁿ solvers. `march` consumes a `Family` (`solvers/march.ts`): `param(x)` reads the marched modulus
coordinate off the point (pushing to ℝ³ⱽ, then `develop.ts`), and `held(x, s)` rebuilds and pulls
`[flat, modulusWall(torus, push(x), s)]` **at the current point** — that rebuild is the per-step
re-freeze. Everything else is reused: `conditions/modulus`'s `tau` + `modulusWall`, `solvers/project`
(the corrector), `conditions/embedded`'s `embedded` (pulled to the gate), `solvers/march` (the loop).
